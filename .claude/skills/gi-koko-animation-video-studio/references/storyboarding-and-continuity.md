# Storyboarding, visual continuity and shot design

## Shot grammar
For each scene specify: scene ID, duration, story purpose, shot size, camera angle, camera movement, subject/action, environment, lighting, color, sound, transition, asset source and subtitle-safe region.

Use a deliberate mix of:
- Establishing wide shots for geography and context.
- Medium shots for action and character interaction.
- Close-ups for emotion and important details.
- Inserts for objects, maps, documents and evidence.
- Cutaways to maintain pacing and hide edits.
- Moving shots only when motivated by subject or story.

## Continuity bible
Create a continuity sheet for recurring characters and locations. Lock character age range, face/hair, clothing, body proportions, props, palette, environment, time of day and lighting. For each generated shot reuse the same reference images and concise character description. Keep screen direction, eyelines, prop positions and time progression consistent.

## Documentary integrity
Separate verified facts, interpretation, reconstruction and fictional dramatization. Label reenactments when a viewer could mistake them for actual footage. Never fabricate archive footage, interviews, quotes, statistics or source attributions. Maintain a source ledger with claim, source URL/title, publication date, access date and scene using it.

## Pacing
Open with a clear hook, establish the central question, build context, deliver evidence or story beats, then resolve with a concise ending. Vary shot length intentionally. Avoid random transitions and excessive zooms.
